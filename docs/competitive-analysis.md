# TrailArc — Competitive Analysis

*October 2026. Based on web research of public sources; private-company figures
are third-party estimates and flagged as such.*

## Bottom line

1. **The whole bundle doesn't exist, but most of its pieces do — and the biggest
   piece is owned by a well-funded incumbent.** Trail discovery, legality,
   private-land *boundaries*, offline maps and OEM bundles are onX Offroad's core
   business, and onX already partners with Toyota, Ford, Jeep — and INEOS.
2. **The real white space is bookable, insured access to private land for
   full-size 4×4s.** LandTrust and Hipcamp proved the model for hunting and
   camping; nobody we found does it for vehicles.
3. **The plan currently mixes two different businesses** — a landowner
   marketplace and an OEM owner-engagement service — with different customers,
   revenue models and risks. They should be split (see "Two concepts" below).

## Landscape

### 1. Off-road navigation & legality — *crowded, dominated by onX*

| Player | What it does | Relevance to TrailArc |
|---|---|---|
| **onX Offroad** (onXmaps) | Trail maps, difficulty, offline, land-ownership boundaries for ~147M private parcels (Elite, $99.99/yr). 3M+ subscribers across six onX apps; est. ~$1.4B valuation (CB Insights, unverified); new growth round led by TCV (Nov 2025). | Direct overlap with TrailArc's "legal trail discovery" pillar. Shows who owns land — does **not** sell access to it. |
| **onX OEM deals** | Toyota (6 months Elite with 2026 trucks; Trail Revival stewardship events), Ford (onX Offroad/Hunt integration, 2026), Jeep (since 2021), **INEOS "50 States, 50 Trails" (2026)**. | onX is the default OEM navigation partner, *including INEOS*. Competing with onX for this slot is a losing fight; partnering is plausible. |
| **Gaia GPS** (Outside) | Map layers, route planning; overlander favorite. | Navigation commodity. |
| **Trails Offroad** | Curated trail guides, $39.99/yr; used alongside onX for planning. | Content commodity. |

**Implication:** drop "aviation-grade maps" and legality as headline
differentiators. Integrate or partner (onX, Gaia, USFS data) instead of rebuilding.

### 2. Private-land access marketplaces — *proven model, empty for 4×4s*

| Player | What it does | Relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **LandTrust** (Bozeman, MT; Series A) | "Airbnb for private land": hunting, fishing, ranch tours. 20% commission, landowner keeps 80%, no contract. Handles vetting, billing, $1M general liability + guest medical coverage. ~600 properties / 1M+ acres, mostly Rocky Mountain West. Off-roading appears only as an incidental activity. | **Closest analog — essentially TrailArc's landowner model for a different activity.** Could expand into vehicles; also a possible partner/acquirer. |
| **Hipcamp** | Private-land camping marketplace, 565K sites (2025). $1M per-occurrence host liability, excess to host's own policy. | Proves host-insurance marketplace at scale; camping, not driving. |
| **Harvest Hosts** | Membership for RV stays at farms/wineries. | Adjacent, membership model. |
| **Spearhead Trails** (SW Virginia) / **Hatfield-McCoy** (WV) | Public-authority-run paid trail systems on private/corporate land (~600 mi in VA), primarily ATV/UTV. | The incumbent Appalachian model for paid motorized access on private land — a competitor for riders, a model for agreements, and a cautionary tale (15+ environmental violations at Spearhead). |
| Private OHV parks & events (e.g. HyperFest 4×4 pass, Rausch Creek) | Single-site day passes. | Fragmented supply TrailArc could aggregate. |

**No dedicated marketplace for booking private-land 4×4 trail access was found.**
This is TrailArc's clearest opening.

### 3. OEM owner-experience programs — *each big brand builds its own*

| Program | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Ford Bronco Off-Roadeo** | Owner driving experiences at 5 sites (TX, UT, NV, NH, TN), complimentary visit with a new Bronco; "Bring Your Own Bronco" days in 2026. |
| **Jeep Badge of Honor** (since 2013) | App with trail check-ins, badges, difficulty ratings, photo sharing; physical badges. Same gamification idea as TrailArc. |
| **Land Rover Experience**, Toyota Trail Teams | Driving schools / brand ambassador programs *(known programs; not re-verified in this research pass)*. |
| **INEOS today** | Owner gatherings (155+ Grenadiers in Moab, May 2025), dealer events with aftermarket partners, onX partnership. No dedicated owners app found. |

**Implication:** badges + skills days are table stakes for large OEMs, who build
them in-house. The opening is **smaller or newer brands that can't**
(INEOS, and potentially others entering off-road).

### 4. Stewardship & advocacy
Tread Lightly!, BlueRibbon Coalition, RealTruck's Rugged Ridge Trail Access
grants (~$10–20K/yr), onX + Toyota Trail Revival. Good partners for credibility;
onX already owns much of the stewardship narrative.

## Does the solution already exist?

| TrailArc pillar | Exists? | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Legal trail discovery, offline maps | **Yes — mature** | onX, Gaia, Trails Offroad |
| Private-land ownership data | **Yes** | onX Elite |
| *Bookable* private-land access for 4×4s, with waivers + insurance | **No** (closest: LandTrust for hunting, Hipcamp for camping) | — |
| Badges / gamification | Yes, per-brand | Jeep Badge of Honor |
| OEM owner events & skills | Yes, for large OEMs | Ford, Jeep, Land Rover |
| White-label owner-experience platform for smaller OEMs | **Not found** | — |
| AR repair / VR training | Early, fragmented | (deferred anyway) |

## Two concepts — the recommended split

The 2025 plan blends these, which is why it reads as muddled. They differ on
every axis:

| | **A. TrailArc Access** — landowner marketplace | **B. TrailArc for Brands** — OEM owner engagement |
|---|---|---|
| One-liner | "LandTrust for 4×4s": book insured access to private trails | Turnkey owner-experience program for vehicle brands |
| Customer who pays | Explorers (booking fee) + landowners (commission) | OEMs / dealers (contracts) |
| Revenue | 15–20% take rate, later subscriptions | Annual program fees, per-event fees, data reports |
| Core asset | Landowner supply + insurance/waiver product | Event ops, content, dealer integration |
| Must be | **Brand-neutral** — open to every make | Brand-specific by design |
| Main risk | Liability & insurance for motorized use; two-sided cold start | Services-heavy, lumpy B2B sales; onX already in the room |
| Investor story | Venture-scale marketplace with a clear analog | Profitable services/SaaS business; smaller multiple |
| Role for INEOS | A *channel partner* (sends owners), not an owner | The *first customer* |

**Why the split matters:**

- **Neutrality.** The INEOS proposal offers INEOS 20–25% equity, a right of first
  refusal and priority data access. That makes the marketplace look like an INEOS
  property — a reason for Toyota, Ford or Jeep owners (and their OEMs) to stay
  away. The marketplace needs every brand's owners to work.
- **Different pitches.** Landowners care about insurance and income. INEOS cares
  about retention, dealer service revenue and U.S. brand building. One deck can't
  serve both well.
- **Different proof points.** A needs bookings and hosts; B needs a signed OEM
  program and attendance numbers.

**How they connect:** B can feed A. OEM programs send owners to bookable
properties, and branded events run on A's land. But A should stand alone.

## Strategic recommendations

1. **Lead with A (the marketplace) as the company.** It is the defensible,
   venture-scale white space. Position onX as a partner ("book it in TrailArc,
   navigate it in onX"), not a target.
2. **Run B as a partner program, not the core thesis.** Pitch INEOS as a paying
   program customer and channel, with no exclusivity and no ROFR. If INEOS invests,
   keep it a minority stake without control rights over a brand-neutral marketplace.
3. **Solve insurance first.** Hipcamp and LandTrust policies are written for
   camping and hunting; motorized use is typically excluded or priced differently.
   Also check Virginia's recreational-use statute: in most states, landowner
   immunity applies only when access is **free**, so paid access may remove that
   protection. This is the gating legal question for A.
4. **Pick a wedge LandTrust and onX don't serve.** Full-size 4×4 owners in the
   East (Virginia/Appalachia), where public trails are scarce and private land
   dominates — the reverse of LandTrust's western, hunting-heavy footprint.
5. **Watch LandTrust.** It is the most likely fast follower into vehicles, and a
   plausible partner or acquirer.
